1What we collect

Your account. The email address you sign up with, and the name you give us. If you use Sign in with Google instead, Google tells us your name and email address so that the account can be created.

What you put in the app. Trips, destinations and dates; itinerary entries, stays and transport; expenses, budgets and who owes what; packing lists; journal entries and any photos you attach to them; travel documents you upload or import; and the profile details you fill in, such as your home country, emergency contact and loyalty numbers.

Notification tokens. If you turn on trip notifications, we store a push token for that device so a reminder can reach it. Turning notifications off stops it being used.

Your location, only when you ask for it. The trip map in Explore can show where you are. Travel Buddy asks for location permission the first time you tap to show your position on that map, and never before; you can allow it — on recent Android versions, as precise or approximate location — or say no, and the map works either way. Your position is used only while that map is open on your screen, and only to draw it there. The app never stores your position, on your device or on our servers, and never sends it to us, includes it in usage statistics, or puts it in a crash report. There is no background tracking of any kind: when the map is closed, the app does not look at where you are. Nothing else in the app uses your position — your trip's days and places always come from what you planned, not from where you happen to be.

Usage statistics. Unless you turn them off, the app records a small, fixed set of product events — for example that the app was opened, that a booking import succeeded or failed, or that a day of a trip in progress was viewed — so that we can tell whether a feature actually helps. Each event carries only details chosen in advance — labels from a closed list (such as which screen it happened on), yes-or-no flags (such as whether the phone was offline) and a few counts (such as which day of the trip it is, or how many fields of an import were edited) — together with the time it happened, a random identifier created for this installation of the app, the app version and the platform. It never carries your name, email address or account identifier, the names of your trips or places, anything you typed, your location, or the contents of a booking, journal entry, document or photo.

2How we use it

We use what you save to run the app: to show you your trips, to keep them readable on your device when you have no signal, and to sync your changes back when you do.

We use it to send the trip notifications you switched on — a check-in opening, a departure coming up, a budget you asked to be warned about. Every one of them is worked out from something already in your own trip.

We use the usage statistics described above only to understand which parts of the app are used and whether they work. Apart from crash reports (section 3), they are the only measurement the app sends: there is no advertising or attribution SDK, no recording of your screen or session replay, no heatmaps, and no automatic capture of what you tap. You can turn usage statistics off at any time under Profile › Privacy & Security; the app then stops recording them and discards any it has not yet sent.

We also count activity in the service's own records — for example how many entries were added to trips each day — to see whether the app is being used; those counts come from what you already saved, not from any extra tracking.

We do not use your data for advertising and we do not build an advertising or marketing profile of you. We do not sell your personal data.

3Who else is involved

Running the app means a small number of companies receive some of your data. This is the complete list.

Supabase hosts the service — the database your trips live in, the private file storage that holds your documents and journal photos, and the sign-in system that holds your email address and password.

Expo, which runs the push service the app is built on, receives each device's push token and the text of each notification, and passes them to Google for delivery. To set up notifications on a device, the app sends Expo the token Google's Firebase service issued for that device, a random identifier Expo creates for this installation of the app, and the app's own identifiers. With each notification, our server also sends Expo the identifiers the app needs to open the right screen when you tap it. A notification's text can include details from your trip, such as a flight or a check-in time.

Google delivers push notifications to Android devices through Firebase Cloud Messaging, and handles Sign in with Google if you choose it.

Anthropic reads your booking imports. When you import a confirmation, the file or text you chose is sent through our server to Anthropic's Claude API, which pulls the flight, stay or booking details out of it. The app sends it only to get that answer.

Mapbox powers the search for destinations and cities. What you type into a destination, city, journal-location, wishlist or transport city field — or into a pickup or meeting-point field on an imported booking — is sent to Mapbox to return suggestions, and the name and country of a destination are sent to Mapbox when the app needs to find that destination on a map.

Geoapify draws the trip map in Explore and helps find the places your plans name. It supplies the map images for the area you are looking at, so it receives requests for that area — if you centre the map on yourself, that area is around you, though your exact position is never sent. When you search for the place of a plan or the address of a stay — including while reviewing an import — what you type is sent to Geoapify as you type, together with the public map position of the trip's city when the app knows it, to suggest places. When a plan has a location you typed but no saved position, that text and the city it belongs to (with the city's public map position) are sent to Geoapify to find it, so the app can offer directions there. To estimate how long it takes to walk between two of your planned places, the app sends Geoapify the map positions of those two places and nothing else about them — and only for places found through Geoapify or published map data, never a place picked through the Mapbox search and never your own position. The walking times that come back are kept on your device with your account's other offline data. No account details are sent with any of these requests, though like any web service Geoapify sees the network address a request comes from.

PostHog receives the usage statistics described in section 1, on servers in the European Union. They are sent under the random identifier for this installation of the app, never under your account, and they stop entirely if you turn usage statistics off.

WeatherAPI.com returns forecasts. We send it the destination of the trip you are looking at — nothing about you.

Frankfurter supplies exchange rates. It receives currency codes only, and nothing about you or your trip.

AeroDataBox, through RapidAPI, supplies live flight status. Our server checks the flights in your trips on a schedule: for a flight that has an airline code and flight number, it looks up that flight a few times in the day before it departs, and more often in the last three hours before departure until shortly after it leaves. Each time, it sends AeroDataBox that flight number and the local date of departure, and nothing that identifies you, your account or the trip the flight belongs to. This happens whether or not you have notifications switched on.

Sentry receives crash reports on builds where error reporting is switched on. A report carries the error message, the app version, the platform and your account identifier — never your trips, journal entries, documents or photos. A report that cannot be sent is discarded rather than stored.

Nobody else. Travel Buddy has no connection to travel or accommodation providers such as Airbnb or Booking.com, and sends them nothing. An "open in maps" or Directions action hands a place — its address, or its position on the map — to Google Maps outside the app, and only when you tap it. The same is true of a booking link: if your confirmation printed the web address of your reservation, we store that address as part of the booking and hand it to your browser when you tap it. Your browser then makes an ordinary visit to that page, exactly as it would if you had typed the address yourself. We never build such a link, and never open one on your behalf.

4Where it is kept, and how it is protected

Your data is stored on servers operated by Supabase. Traffic between the app and those servers is encrypted in transit using TLS.

Journal photos and travel documents are held in private storage. They have no public address: every request for one is checked against your account first, and the link the app uses to fetch a file is short-lived.

Which rows your account may read or write is enforced by the database itself, through row-level security policies, rather than only by the app asking politely. That is what keeps one traveller out of another traveller’s trip even if a request is malformed or replayed.

Travel Buddy also keeps a copy of your trips on your device, so the app works with no connection. That copy lives in the app’s private storage and goes when you uninstall the app.

No service can promise perfect security, and we are not going to. If we become aware of a breach affecting your data, we will tell you.

5Trips you share

A trip can have more than one member. When you invite somebody to a trip, they can see what that trip holds — its itinerary, stays, transport, expenses and packing list — and, depending on the role you give them, change it. Entries carry the name of the member who created them, so the group can tell who added what.

This is the one place your content reaches another person, and it happens because you invited them. Your journal entries and their photos are yours alone; trip membership does not share them.

Removing a member ends their access to the trip from that point on.

6Your rights

You have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal data at any time.

To request an export, use Download My Data under Profile › Privacy & Security — this generates a file of your own data for you to save or share. To delete your account and its data, use Delete Account on your Profile page. If either cannot complete on your device, we open a message to our support address instead, and a person reads and processes it within 5 business days.

7Children

Travel Buddy is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child has created an account, write to the address below and we will remove it.
